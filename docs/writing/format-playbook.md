## 1. Where the evidence comes from

The patterns below were derived from a full extraction of a competing South African budgeting-app account: 268 posts published over 92 days, 4.99M total views, 2,639 carousel slides read individually. It is one account, so treat these as strong signals rather than laws — but they are signals from a real account in exactly this niche, not general social media advice.

Two structural facts about that account are worth absorbing before anything else:

- **264 of 268 posts were photo carousels.** The four actual videos averaged under 2K views. In this niche, on this audience, static slides outperform video — probably because the content is numbers, and numbers reward re-reading and pausing.
- **The distribution is brutally top-heavy.** Median post: ~2,900 views. Top post: 699,000. Five posts carried roughly a third of all views. This means the strategy is volume plus variance: post often, accept that most posts do modestly, and make sure the format is capable of a breakout. Do not judge the format on a single post.

## 2. Slide architecture

The reliable shape is five movements. Total 12–16 slides.

**Slide 1 — The hook.** Big type, three or four short lines, containing the number.

```
How I spend
my R28 000
p/m salary
South Africa
```

That's it. No preamble, no "hey guys". The number and the country. The country tag matters more than it looks — it signals to the viewer that these prices are their prices.

**Slide 2 — The persona.** A short factual block that lets the viewer place themselves relative to this person.

```
Living in SA
Female, 29
Single
Job: Software engineer
Net salary: R78 000 p/m
```

Include age, gender, relationship/household status, job, and net (take-home) salary. Net, not gross — gross invites "but what about tax" comments and is less relatable. Add city or province when cost of living is central to the post.

**Slides 3 to N — The line items.** One slide per expense category. This is the body of the post and where most of the swipes happen. Each slide:

```
Transport costs
R3 500 p/m

Includes:
Fuel / tracking device / insurance / tolls / maintenance

I fully own the car now, and I'm glad it's fuel efficient —
this number was closer to R6 000 when I was still paying it off.
```

Category name, amount, an "Includes:" line that breaks it down, and one or two lines of first-person commentary. The commentary is not decoration — it's the reason the viewer keeps swiping instead of leaving after the number. Order the categories roughly largest to smallest, or in the order money actually leaves the account (rent, transport, food, debt, insurance, subscriptions, savings, fun).

**The reckoning slide.** After the line items, one slide that totals it up and says what's left.

```
What's left
R4 200

Out of R28 000. Every month I tell myself
I'll save it. Every month something happens.
```

This slide is what turns a list into a story. It's also the most-screenshotted one.

**The close.** One slide for Beyond Horizon, and it should describe what the app did rather than command a download.

```
I stopped guessing where my money goes

I uploaded my statement to Beyond Horizon and it
sorted every transaction into categories in seconds.
No bank login needed.

beyondhorizon.app — free to start
```

Keep it to one slide. Two is an ad.

### Slide count

More slides do better. From the sample: 12+ slides → median 4,395 views; 9–11 → 3,086; 8 or fewer → 2,157. Roughly double the median from the shortest to the longest bucket. The mechanism is presumably watch time — each swipe is more time on the post. **Aim for 12–16.** If a topic only supports 8 slides, it's probably two topics or the wrong topic.

## 3. What the numbers say

Signals from the 268-post sample. Effect sizes are medians, so they're robust to the one viral post.

| Signal | Evidence | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Rand amount in the title** | Titles containing a R figure: median 3,945 views. Titles without: 2,244. | Put a number in the hook. Always. |
| **Question in the caption** | Captions with a question mark: median 6,098 (n=29). Without: 2,764. | End the caption with a real question. Biggest single lever in the data. |
| **Slide count** | 12+ slides: 4,395. ≤8: 2,157. | Go long. 12–16. |
| **Five hashtags** | 5 tags: median 4,585. 3 tags: 2,276. | Use 5. |
| **Lower income personas** | Personas under R40k: median 4,659. R40k+: 3,846. | Skew relatable. Most of the audience earns under R40k. |
| **Sound choice** | Licensed tracks: median 3,342. Original sounds: 2,731. | Inconclusive and contradicts common advice — don't over-think sound. Use a current SA amapiano/house track at low volume; the content is read, not heard. |
| **Generic advice posts** | "Hard money truths", "how to stop your salary disappearing" — low thousands, well below the account median for concrete breakdowns. | Avoid. Every post needs a specific person and specific numbers. |

A caution on all of the above: these are correlations within one account's output, with small samples in some cells (the question-mark finding rests on 29 posts). They're worth acting on because they're cheap to act on, not because they're proven causal.

## 4. Caption, hashtags and sound

**Caption.** One short first-person line that adds emotional framing the slides don't have, then the hashtags. The line should sound like a person muttering, not a brand announcing.

Good: "Is this valid or am I mad?" · "I need help guys" · "This month got away from me" · "Would you have done the same?" · "Be honest — is R3 500 on transport normal in Joburg?"

Bad: "Check out our latest budgeting breakdown! 💰📊" — reads as an ad, kills reach.

End with a question wherever it's not forced. It's the strongest signal in the data and it costs nothing.

**Hashtags.** Five. Mix one broad reach tag, two category tags, and two long-tail specific ones:

- Broad: `#southafrica`
- Category: `#salary` `#budgeting` `#spendingbreakdown` `#money` `#debt`
- Long-tail: `#howispendmysalary` `#budgetingforsouthafricans` `#salarybreakdown` `#livingcostsSA` `#debtfreesa`

Don't include the brand hashtag in every post — it makes the feed look like a campaign. Roughly one in three.

**Sound.** Pick something current and South African at low volume. The content is read, not heard, so the sound's job is only to avoid feeling dead and to pick up any algorithmic benefit from a rising track. Specify a vibe in the brief ("mid-tempo amapiano, nothing with a vocal hook that competes with reading") rather than naming a specific track, since track availability changes.

## 5. Content wells

The account this format was learned from sustained ~3 posts a day for three months from a handful of repeatable wells. These are the wells, ordered roughly by how well they performed, adapted to what Beyond Horizon can credibly talk about.

**Salary breakdowns.** "How I spend my R[X] p/m salary." The backbone. Vary income (R8k student allowance up to R200k executive), job (teacher, nurse, software engineer, artisan, call centre agent, small business owner), city, and household. Effectively unlimited.

**Household and couple budgets.** "How we budget our R127 800 household income." How couples split costs, family-of-five grocery bills, single-parent budgets. Strong comment driver because everyone has an opinion about how couples should split money.

**Comparisons.** "Doctor vs teacher — who spends better?" "R25k vs R45k lifestyle." Two personas side by side, same categories, different outcomes. Built-in argument, built-in comments.

**Debt journeys.** "How I'm clearing R180 000 of debt." Avalanche vs snowball with the actual interest maths, months saved, what got cut. Directly showcases the debt feature and the audience is highly motivated.

**Big one-off events.** Weddings, matric dances, funerals, proposals, birthdays, moving flat, buying a first car. "How we got married on a R200k budget" was a top-five performer on the source account. High shareability, seasonal.

**Cost of ownership.** "What a Polo actually costs per month." Car, home, pet, baby, gym, medical aid — the total real monthly cost including the parts people forget. Naturally listy, naturally long.

**Money leaks.** "You're wasting R3 000 a month." Debit orders, forgotten subscriptions, bank fees, delivery apps. Maps perfectly onto recurring-expense and anomaly detection. Keep it specific — a named leak with a named amount, not "cut back on takeaways".

**Month in review.** "Where my salary actually went last month." Budget vs actual, the confession format. The most direct showcase of statement upload and budget-vs-actual.

**Rankings and listicles.** "Most expensive suburbs in SA", "highest paying jobs in SA". Big reach, weak conversion — they pull an audience that isn't there for budgeting. Use sparingly, maybe one in ten, purely for reach.

**Product-led.** A real walkthrough: uploading a statement, the anomaly alert firing, a 30-day streak. Keep these to roughly one in five posts and make sure they still contain someone's actual numbers, or they read as ads and underperform.

## 7. Worked example

A compact version of a strong post, to calibrate tone. (Full-length would run 14 slides.)

**Slide 1**
> How I spend
> my R31 000
> p/m salary
> South Africa

**Slide 2**
> Living in Pretoria
> Male, 27
> Single, no kids
> Job: Junior civil engineer
> Net salary: R31 000 p/m

**Slide 3**
> Rent
> R7 200 p/m
>
> Includes: 1 bed in Hatfield, water, refuse
>
> Prepaid electricity is separate and it hurts more than the rent some months.

**Slide 7**
> Black tax
> R2 500 p/m
>
> Goes to: my mom's household, my sister's transport
>
> Non-negotiable. I've never once thought of it as a line item until I saw it on a statement.

**Slide 11**
> Eating out
> R2 800 p/m
>
> Includes: work lunches, weekend, delivery apps
>
> I told myself it was maybe R1 200. It was R2 800. That's the one that got me.

**Slide 13**
> What's left
> R1 950
>
> Out of R31 000. On paper I'm fine. In practice I'm one flat tyre from a bad month.

**Slide 14**
> I stopped guessing
>
> I uploaded three months of statements to Beyond Horizon and it
> sorted every transaction for me. The eating-out number was the shock.
>
> No bank login needed. beyondhorizon.app — free to start

**Caption**
> The eating out number genuinely embarrassed me. Be honest — what's yours?

**Hashtags**
> #southafrica #salary #howispendmysalary #spendingbreakdown #budgetingforsouthafricans

Note what's doing the work: the R2 800 confession, the black tax line that most budget content omits, and the closing question that asks for a specific number back.
