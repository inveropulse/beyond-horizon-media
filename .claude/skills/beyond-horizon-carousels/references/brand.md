# Beyond Horizon — product, voice and guardrails

Everything here is fact about the product. When writing content, don't contradict it, and don't invent features that aren't listed.

## What the product is

Beyond Horizon is a personal finance PWA built by **Inveropulse** for the South African market. Website: https://beyondhorizon.app

The one-line positioning from the site: *"Your money — clear, connected, and always up to date."*

The sharper internal positioning, which should drive content: **Money Rhythm is the hook, not budgeting.** Nobody searches "I need a budgeting app". They search for a way to stop running out of money before month end. Content should lead with the feeling of not knowing where the money went, and let the app be the answer.

## Features that actually exist

Four feature areas are live. Content may reference these and nothing else.

**Money Rhythm** — a daily check-in where the user logs where they stand, building a calendar view and a streak. Scheduled money events (debit orders, payday, rent) sit on the calendar. Pro adds balance forecasting — seeing where the balance is heading — and a streak freeze.

**Transactions & Insights** — the user uploads a bank statement and the app parses it into categorised transactions. Spending trend chart, category donut, top expenses, anomaly detection ("we found 3 unusual charges"), recurring expense detection, AI spending suggestions, cohort benchmarks (how you compare to similar users), CSV/PDF export.

**Budget** — monthly budgets by category, progress tracking, budget vs actual, copy last month's budget, import actuals straight from an uploaded statement.

**Debt Management** — track debts, compare avalanche vs snowball payoff strategies, payoff projections, total interest cost comparison.

**Across everything** — gamification (XP, levels, streaks, quests) and a privacy mode that hides the numbers on screen. Both are free forever.

### The differentiator worth repeating

**No bank login required.** The user uploads a statement; they never hand over banking credentials. In the SA market this is a genuine trust advantage and a high-intent search term. It's a feature, not an apology — lead with it when trust or security comes up.

Trust lines that belong on any pricing mention: **cancel anytime**, **your data is always yours**. SA audiences are sceptical of subscriptions; these do real work.

Never paywalled, and safe to promise in content: the daily check-in, viewing transactions from a statement you already uploaded, categorising transactions, privacy mode, account deletion and full data export, all gamification.

## Audience

Primary segment is **24–34, South African**, earning and employed, financially aware but not financially sorted. They know they should track their money and haven't managed to make it stick. They are on TikTok and Instagram Reels; they're in SA personal finance Facebook groups; they are sceptical of anything that smells like a bank.

Secondary: 35–45 with dependants, debt and more complexity — responds to the debt payoff and household budget content.

Currency is rands, always written as **R28 000** or **R28,000** consistently within a post. Use SA terms: petrol not gas, medical aid not health insurance, salary not paycheck, debit order not direct debit, matric, taxi fare, load shedding, prepaid electricity, UIF, SARS, TymeBank/Capitec/FNB/Discovery as ordinary references.

## Voice

Write like a candid friend who is good with numbers and bad at pretending. Specifically:

- **First person, present tense, plain.** "I spend R3 500 on transport" not "transport expenditure amounts to".
- **Confessional, not instructional.** The persona reports what they did, including the parts that were dumb. They don't tell the reader what to do.
- **Dry, not jokey.** A wry aside lands; a punchline doesn't. "I fully own the car now, and I'm glad it's fuel efficient" is the register.
- **No hype.** No "game changer", "life-changing", "you won't believe". The numbers are interesting on their own; decorating them makes them less credible.
- **No emoji spam.** One or two at most, and only where a person would actually use one.
- **Short lines.** These are read on a phone at speed, one slide per second. If a line wraps to three lines on a phone, cut it.

The house rule that keeps the voice honest: **if it wouldn't survive a comment section of South Africans checking your arithmetic, don't write it.**

## Guardrails

### Personas are illustrative
The people in these carousels are composites built to represent a real situation. Never present one as an actual Beyond Horizon user, never write an invented testimonial, never attach a real name or photo. If the brief includes real user data, confirm it's anonymised and that the user consented — then say in the post that figures are shared with permission.

### Not financial advice
South Africa regulates financial advice (FSCA / FAIS). Describing what a fictional person spent is content. Telling the audience what they should do with their money is advice. Stay on the content side:

- Fine: "She puts R5 000 into a tax-free savings account each month." (Describing.)
- Not fine: "You should be putting R5 000 into a TFSA." (Directing.)
- Not fine: any projected return, "guaranteed", "risk-free", or naming a specific product to buy.
- Fine: "Ask a registered financial adviser" as a closing line where the topic warrants it.

### Product honesty
Don't claim bank-feed connections, credit scores, tax filing, investment brokerage or anything else not in the feature list above. If a content idea needs a feature that doesn't exist, say so and propose an alternative rather than writing around it.

### Debt content
Debt is the most emotionally loaded topic in this niche. Show the arithmetic (interest cost, payoff order, months saved) and skip the shame. No "get out of debt fast", no implied judgment of people in debt — that audience is the one most likely to convert and the most likely to leave if talked down to.

### Competitors
There are direct competitors in this space posting the same format. Don't name them, don't subtweet them, don't do comparison posts. Compete by being more specific, not by being louder.
